# The Route From This Diploma To A First Job

**Read this in week 1. Fifteen minutes. Not in month nine.**

Everything on this page was read from its source on **3 September 2026**. Every quote is word-for-word from the page named beside it. Where a source could not be reached, this page says so instead of filling the hole.

You are about to spend nine months and 420 contact hours on this diploma. This page tells you, before you start, exactly which doors that certificate opens, which one it does not open, and what the state has built to get you through the one it does not.

---

## 1. What your certificate is, in the framework's own words

Saudi Arabia's National Qualifications Framework places qualifications on numbered levels. This diploma sits at **Level 4**.

The framework's own definition:

> "An Associate Diploma is awarded upon successful completion of a minimum of 24 credit hours (units) after secondary level education or its equivalent. ... It takes one full-time academic year, or its equivalent to complete in accordance with the regulations and guidelines organizing it. **It is designed to develop the knowledge and skills necessary for employment in an administrative or vocational fields that require limited specialized experience.** This degree is considered a qualification by itself."
>
> — *National Qualifications Framework in The Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (NQF - KSA), Second Edition, 1444 AH – 2023 AD*, published by ETEC. Emphasis added.

The qualification titles the framework gives to the levels around yours, quoted exactly:

| Level | Qualification title in the framework |
|-------|--------------------------------------|
| 4 | "Associate Diploma or Equivalent" |
| 5 | "Intermediate Diploma or equivalent" |
| 6 | "Bachelor's Degree or Equivalent" |

Read that table once and accept it. A Level 4 associate diploma is **not** a bachelor's degree in the Saudi framework, and no covering letter, portfolio or interview turns it into one. Anywhere a hiring filter says "bachelor's degree", it is a wall and not a preference. Knowing that in week 1 costs you nothing. Discovering it in month ten costs you the ten months you spent believing otherwise.

**Note on the source:** ETEC's own server would not serve this PDF to us on 3 September 2026 — the document URL returned the ETEC homepage instead of the file. The copy quoted above was obtained from a Saudi university's public mirror of the same ETEC document. That mirrored copy carries the stamp `تحت المراجعة` ("under review") 39 times across its 44 pages. The level titles above are stated identically in three separate places in that document, so treat them as reliable; treat any figure you take from its multi-row credit-hour tables as worth re-checking against ETEC directly before you rely on it in writing.

## 2. The wall, with a real posting

On 3 September 2026, a search of Amazon's public job API for "Riyadh" returned **46 matches**, of which the first page of **16 were all Riyadh postings**. One of those 16 is the graduate-entry role — the one designed for people with no experience, on the Saudi national hiring track:

> **Graduate Area Manager (KSA National)** — Riyadh, SAU. Job ID `9ea4fc34-3e34-4abd-a898-2419195833a5`, posted 12 February 2026.
> Basic qualifications, word for word (the source separates the bullets with HTML line breaks; nothing else is changed):
> "- University degree (Bachelor's or Master's degree), with a graduation date within the last 24 months.
> - Advanced proficiency in written and verbal English and local language
> - Relevant experience in performing data analysis"

Line one closes the door. Lines two and three are what the next nine months are for — everything in this diploma is in English, and "performing data analysis" is the whole subject. You will meet this shape often: **you can do the job and still fail the filter.**

The same pattern globally. Searching Amazon worldwide for its entry-grade data science title on 3 September 2026:

> `"Data Scientist I"` → **1 posting in the world** (São Paulo, Brazil).
> Its basic qualifications end: "Bachelor's degree or above in Math, Statistics, Computer Science, or related Science field".

One entry-grade posting worldwide, and it is degree-gated. This is the honest picture. The entry rung at large multinationals is both rare and gated.

## 3. The door, with a real posting

The same employer, one country over, a different door:

> **Data Analyst - UAE National, ICQA/LND Analytics Team** — Dubai, ARE. Job ID `d7495bc1-0278-45bc-9c97-7082832137f9`, posted 26 August 2026.
> Basic qualifications, word for word, all five of them:
> "- Experience that includes strong analytical skills, attention to detail, and effective communication abilities
> - Experience communicating clearly and concisely with leadership, stakeholders, and cross-functional teams
> - Experience managing and executing large projects from start to finish, including project management, data gathering and modeling, problem solving, and communicating recommendations
> - Experience understanding needs of business and end customers and translating them into right solutions
> - Experience with AWS technologies like Redshift, S3, AWS Glue, EMR, Kinesis, FireHose, Lambda, and IAM roles and permissions"

Read what is **not** there: no degree, and no number of years. Five bullets, and four of them are about explaining things to people. Read what **is** there: named data tooling you can learn, and "communicating clearly and concisely with leadership".

This is the shape of the door that is open to you. It is a national-hiring track, it is skill-stated, and the skills it states are teachable in nine months.

**Be honest about the limit of this example.** This posting is in the UAE, not Saudi Arabia. We could not verify a Saudi posting with the same shape on 3 September 2026: the regional job boards (Bayt, GulfTalent, NaukriGulf) refuse automated reading, and among the 16 Riyadh postings we could read, not one was an entry-grade data or ML role. Do not read this page as proof that Riyadh is full of skill-stated junior data jobs. Read it as proof that this shape of posting exists in the region and is worth hunting for by name.

## 4. Tamheer: the route the state built for exactly your problem

You have no years of experience. Employers ask for years. The Human Resources Development Fund (HRDF, "Hadaf") runs **Tamheer** — *Graduate Development* — to break that loop: it places graduates into a real workplace for a fixed period and pays them itself.

**HRDF's stated conditions, quoted word-for-word from its own application page** (`hrdf.org.sa`, read 3 September 2026):

> - "Must be of Saudi nationality."
> - "Must be registered on the Human Resources Development Fund website."
> - "Must hold a diploma from accredited institutes and technical, health, or administrative colleges, with a duration of at least one academic year (two semesters)."
> - "Should not have previous work experience in the public or private sector exceeding one calendar year."
> - "Age should not exceed 30 calendar years."
> - "Should not be currently employed in the public or private sector."
> - "Should not be listed as 'prohibited' by the Fund."
> - "Should not have benefited from the 'Training Balance' of the Graduates Development Program (TAMHEER) with a full benefit of no more than two opportunities totaling 6 months for the two opportunities."

And from HRDF's programme page for individuals:

> "a monthly financial allowance of three thousand Saudi Riyals" — "a duration ranging from 3 to 6 months"

The third bullet is the one that concerns you: it names **diplomas from technical institutes and colleges of at least one academic year (two semesters)**. HRDF's page for employers describes who it funds as holders of "technical, health, and administrative diplomas, as well as bachelor's degree holders and above" — two separate populations, not one. Applications go through **Taqat**, which HRDF describes as "an umbrella for programs and products offered by the Human Resources Development Fund".

**A caution about the wording.** HRDF's two English pages state the qualification conditions differently, and both list the diploma condition and the bachelor's condition as consecutive bullets that each begin with "must". Read against the Arabic pages and against the employer-side page, they read as alternative qualification routes rather than two things you need at once — but that is our reading of the layout, not a sentence HRDF has written. But the Arabic is the authoritative text, and this page is not it. **Do not apply, or decide not to apply, on the strength of this page.** Open `hrdf.org.sa`, read the conditions in Arabic, and if the diploma bullet's fit to your certificate is not obvious, ask HRDF directly before you assume either way.

### The part nobody tells you: your eligibility is a clock, and it is consumable

Look at those conditions again as a set:

- **It expires.** Age 30 is a hard ceiling.
- **It can be spent by working.** "Should not have previous work experience ... exceeding one calendar year", and "Should not be currently employed". A year in an unrelated job can close this route.
- **It is finite.** At most two opportunities, six months in total, for your whole life.

You do not have an unlimited number of these. That is a decision you should make deliberately in month one, not stumble into in month twelve.

## 5. Tawteen: why an employer would hire someone with no years

Tamheer gets you inside a building. **Tawteen** is why a manager says yes to a permanent contract afterwards. HRDF's own description, quoted word-for-word (`hrdf.org.sa`, read 3 September 2026):

> "It is one of the programs of the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development designed for the employment of job seekers who have **not previously entered the labor market** or have been unemployed in the private sector."

The wage support it offers, quoted word-for-word, is **"From 30% to 50% of the monthly wage* with a maximum of 3,000 Saudi riyals"**, and **"The duration of the Tawteen program will be 24 months for individual workers and 36 months for establishments."**

Read that as a hiring manager reads it. The state pays a share of your salary for up to two years *because* you have never worked before. Your lack of experience is the qualifying condition, not the disqualifying one. When you are sitting in front of someone who says "we were really looking for two years", that is a fact worth being able to state calmly and accurately.

## 6. What this means for the next nine months

Nothing in 420 contact hours manufactures a year of employment. Be clear-eyed about that. Three things actually move the needle, and all three start now, not in month eight:

1. **Keep the Tamheer clock intact and know its shape.** Register on HRDF early so the account exists before you need it. Understand what spends your eligibility.
2. **Build evidence that substitutes for years.** Not a certificate — a public trail. A dozen public repositories — roughly one per course — with commit dates spread across nine months says something a certificate cannot: that you worked steadily, in the open, for nine months. `TOOLING/examples/02_git_for_one_and_for_two.ipynb` and `TOOLING/examples/06_handing_over_a_repository.ipynb`, at the root of this repository, exist to make that trail readable by a stranger.
3. **Hunt the skill-stated postings by name.** The Dubai posting above states no degree and no years. Postings of that shape exist. Read the *basic qualifications* section of every advert before anything else, and learn to tell a wall from a wish.

## 💬 Discuss

You finish the diploma. In month two of looking, a company offers you a full-time job at 7,000 SAR a month. It is not a data role — it is administration, with a little Excel. A Tamheer place in a data team pays 3,000 SAR for six months and does not promise a job at the end.

Take the salary or take the Tamheer place?

Argue it with the conditions in section 4 in front of you, not from instinct. Whoever argues for the salary must say what happens to the "should not be currently employed" and "exceeding one calendar year" conditions. Whoever argues for Tamheer must say what they live on, and what they do in month seven if no offer comes. There is no answer key to this one, and your instructor does not have one either.

## ⚠️ Where this breaks

**The Saudization track is not automatically a degree-free track.** That is the most tempting wrong conclusion to draw from this page, and there is a named, dated case against it: **Amazon's Graduate Area Manager (KSA National)** in Riyadh, job ID `9ea4fc34-3e34-4abd-a898-2419195833a5`, posted 12 February 2026 — a Saudi-national graduate-entry role whose very first basic qualification is "University degree (Bachelor's or Master's degree)". Being Saudi opens a track; it does not delete the degree line inside that track. Some nationalised roles are skill-stated and some are degree-gated, and you find out which by reading the basic qualifications, every time.

**And this page has an expiry date.** Every figure here was read on 3 September 2026 from the pages named. Stipends change, age ceilings change, programmes are renamed and merged. This page is a map of where to look, not a substitute for looking. Before you act on any number here, open the source yourself.
