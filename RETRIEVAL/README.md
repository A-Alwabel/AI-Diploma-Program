# Retrieval — the weekly fifteen minutes that keep every earlier course alive

Thirty-five weekly papers, one per teaching week, ten items each. Taken in class in the last
fifteen minutes of one session a week. **Ungraded.** The instructor works the correct answers
aloud immediately afterwards — that is not optional: with corrective feedback the effect is
g = 0.537, without it 0.374 (Yang, Luo, Vadillo, Yu & Shanks 2021, *Psychological Bulletin*,
222 studies, 48,478 students).

**Why it exists.** The diploma teaches twelve courses strictly in sequence over nine months. A
student finishes Course 01 in week 2 and would otherwise not touch it again before graduating in
month nine. Each paper draws roughly three items from the week just taught, three from about a
month back, and four from the start of the programme, so Course 01 is still being asked about in
week 20.

**What the items are.** Variants built from the unit quizzes and the lessons' own printed
outputs. They never reproduce a final-exam item — that would turn the final into a re-test of
seen items — and the instructor keys record the rule.

**Honest limit.** The meta-analysis behind this has k = 2 for computer science; the effect is
generalised here from psychology and medicine. The instructor's own week-6 cold test on week-2
material is the measurement that decides whether it works for this cohort.

Papers: `week_01.md` … `week_35.md`. Keys live in the instructor repository only.
